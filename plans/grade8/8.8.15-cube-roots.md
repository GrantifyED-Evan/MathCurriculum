# Grade 8 · Unit 8 · Lesson 15: Cube Roots

*Unit 8: Pythagorean Theorem and Irrational Numbers* · Reference `8.8.15` · [Lesson](https://accessim.org/6-8/grade-8/unit-8/section-c/lesson-15?a=teacher) · [Preparation](https://accessim.org/6-8/grade-8/unit-8/section-c/lesson-15/preparation?a=teacher)

**Student goal (post on board):** Let’s compare cube roots.

## Learning goals
- Determine the whole numbers that the cube root of a number lies between, and explain (orally) the reasoning.
- Generalize a process for approximating the value of the cube root of a number, and justify (orally and in writing) that if x^3=n, then x=3√(n).

## Student targets
- When I have a cube root, I can reason about which two whole numbers it is between.

## 5–15 min · Activity 15.2: Cube Root Values
*10 min · Partners · MLR8: Discussion Supports*

**Purpose:** The purpose of this activity is for students to think about cube roots of numbers in relation to the two integer values they are closest to.

**Launch**
- Arrange students in groups of 2. Do not give students access to calculators since the goal of this activity is to reason about the value of cube roots of numbers. Give 2 minutes of quiet work, and follow with a whole-class discussion.

**Students do**
- The value of a cube root of a number lies between two integers. Which are those consecutive whole numbers for the following? Be prepared to explain your reasoning.
- 3√(5)
- 3√(23)
- 3√(81)
- 3√(999)

**Monitor for**
- Monitor for students that multiply non-integers by hand to try and approximate.

**Ask**
- “Does this problem remind anyone of something we have done before?”
- “What strategy did you use to figure out the two whole numbers?”

**Synthesis**
- The purpose of this discussion is for students to share the strategies they used to approximate cube roots by looking at perfect cubes. For each question, ask 1–2 students:
- “What strategy did you use to figure out the two whole numbers?” (I made a list of perfect cubes and then found which two the number was between.)
- During the discussion, make a class display of perfect cubes up to at least 10^3=1,000, or add them to the existing display of perfect squares. This display should be posted in the classroom for the remaining lessons within this unit.

**Access supports**
- Representation: Internalize Comprehension. Begin by asking, “Does this problem remind anyone of something we have done before?”
- Supports accessibility for: Memory, Attention
- MLR8 Discussion Supports. For each observation that is shared, invite students to turn to a partner and restate what they heard using precise mathematical language.
- Advances: Listening, Speaking

## 15–25 min · Activity 15.3: Solutions on a Number Line
*10 min · Partners*

**Purpose:** In this activity, students use rational approximations of irrational numbers to place both rational and irrational numbers on a number line.

**Launch**
- Arrange students in groups of 2. Do not provide access to calculators since the goal of this activity is to reason about the location of irrational numbers on the number line. Give students 2 minutes of quiet work time, and follow with a whole-class discussion.

**Students do**
- The numbers x, y, and z are positive, and:
- x^3= 5
- y^3= 27
- z^3= 700
- [Image: A number line that shows the integers from negative 3 to 9]
- Plot x, y, and z on the number line. Be prepared to share your reasoning with the class.
- Plot - 3√(2) on the number line.

**Synthesis**
- The purpose of this discussion is to make sure students understand how they can use rational numbers to approximate irrational numbers. Display the number line from the Task Statement for all to see. Select 1–2 groups to share their reasoning for each value placement and record their values on the displayed number line. After each placement, ask if any students used different reasoning and invite them to share with the class.

**Access supports**
- Representation: Develop Language and Symbols. Use virtual or concrete manipulatives to connect symbols to concrete objects or values. For example, use a kinesthetic representation of the number line on a clothesline. Students can place and adjust numbers on folder paper or cardstock on the clothesline in a hands-on manner.
- Supports accessibility for: Visual-Spatial Processing, Conceptual Processing

## 25–30 min · Lesson Synthesis
*5 min\* · Whole class*

**Purpose:** The purpose of this discussion is to generalize a process for approximating the value of a cube root by reinforcing that if x^3=n, then x=3√(n).

**Ask**
- “What is the solution to the equation a^3=47?”
- “What is the solution to the equation a^3=125?”
- “What is the solution to the equation a^3=-125?”
- “How can we plot the cube root of any number on the number line?”

**Synthesis**
- The purpose of this discussion is to generalize a process for approximating the value of a cube root by reinforcing that if x^3=n, then x=3√(n). Here are some questions for discussion:
- "What is the solution to the equation a^3=47?” (3√(47))
- "Where would 3√(47) be located on a number line? (3^3=27 and 4^3=64, so 3√(47) has to be between 3 and 4.)
- "What is the solution to the equation a^3=125?” (3√(125)=5)
- “What is the solution to the equation a^3=-125?” (3√(-125)=-5)
- “How can we plot the cube root of any number on the number line?” (Find the two perfect cubes that are closest to the number and use them to determine which integers the cube root of the number will be between and the approximate location between them.)

## Student lesson summary
- Like square roots, most cube roots of whole numbers are irrational. The only time the cube root of a number is a rational number is when the number we are taking the cube root of is a perfect cube. For example, 8 is a perfect cube, and 3√(8)=2.
- We can approximate the values of the cube root of a number by observing the integers around it and remembering the relationship between cubes and cube roots. For example, 3√(20) is between 2 and 3 since 2^3=8 and 3^3=27, and 20 is between 8 and 27. Similarly, since 100 is between 4^3 and 5^3, we know 3√(100) is between 4 and 5.
- Many calculators have a cube root function which can be used to approximate the value of a cube root more precisely. Using our numbers from before, a calculator will show that 3√(20) ≈ 2.7144 and that 3√(100) ≈ 4.6416.
