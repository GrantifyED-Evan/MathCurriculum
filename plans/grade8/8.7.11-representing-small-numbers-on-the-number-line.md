# Grade 8 · Unit 7 · Lesson 11: Representing Small Numbers on the Number Line

*Unit 7: Exponents and Scientific Notation* · Reference `8.7.11` · [Lesson](https://accessim.org/6-8/grade-8/unit-7/section-c/lesson-11?a=teacher) · [Preparation](https://accessim.org/6-8/grade-8/unit-7/section-c/lesson-11/preparation?a=teacher)

**Student goal (post on board):** Let’s visualize small numbers on the number line using powers of 10.

## Learning goals
- Coordinate (orally and in writing) decimals and multiples of powers of 10 representing the same small number.
- Use number lines to represent (orally and in writing) small numbers as multiples of powers of 10 with negative exponents.

## Student targets
- I can plot a multiple of a power of 10 on such a number line.
- I can subdivide and label a number line between 0 and a power of 10 with a negative exponent into 10 equal intervals.
- I can write a small number as a multiple of a power of 10.

## Standards
- **Addressing:** 8.EE.A.1, 8.EE.A.3, 8.EE.A.4

## 0–5 min · Warm-up 11.1: Small Numbers on a Number Line
*5 min · Individual, then share*

**Purpose:** In this Warm-up students reason about expressions with negative exponents on a number line.

**Launch**
- Give students 2 minutes of quiet work time followed by a whole-class discussion.

**Students do**
- Kiran drew this number line.
- [Image: A number line.]
- A number line, 11 tick marks, 0, 1 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 2 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 3 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 4 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 5 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 6 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 7 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 8 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 9 times 10 to the negative 4 power, 10 to the negative 5 power.
- Andre said, “That doesn’t look right to me.”
- Explain why Kiran is correct or explain how he can fix the number line.

**Synthesis**
- The goal of this discussion is to highlight the idea that the larger the size (or absolute value) of a negative exponent, the closer the value of the expression is to zero. This is because the negative exponent indicates the number of factors that are 1/10. For example, 10^-5 represents 5 factors that are 1/10, and 10^-6 represents 6 factors that are 1/10, so 10^-6 is 10 times smaller than 10^-5.
- Invite students to share their reasoning about whether the number line is correct or not. If not brought up in students’ explanations, show at least two correct ways in which the number line can be fixed—by changing the exponents for each tick mark to be one power of 10 smaller than 10^-5 (10^-6), by changing 10^-5 to be one power of 10 greater than the tick marks (10^-3), or by changing all of the exponents so that the tick marks are one power of 10 less than the power of 10 at the end of the number line.

## 5–15 min · Activity 11.2: Comparing Small Numbers on a Number Line
*10 min · Partners · MLR8: Discussion Supports*

**Students do**
- [Image: A number line, 11 tick marks, 0, 9 blank tick marks, 10 to the negative 5 power.]
- Label the tick marks on the number line.
- Plot the following numbers on the number line:
- A. 6 boldcdot 10^ -6
- B. 6 boldcdot 10^ -7
- C. 29 boldcdot 10^ -7
- D. (0.7) boldcdot 10^ -5
- Which is larger, 29 boldcdot 10^ -7 or 6 boldcdot 10^ -6? Estimate how many times larger.
- Which is larger, 7 boldcdot 10^ -8 or 3 boldcdot 10^ -9? Estimate how many times larger.

**Ask**
- “What can 10^-7 be multiplied by to get 10^-6?”
- “If 10^-7 is multiplied by 10 to get 10^-6, the value of the expression will change. What can be done to keep the value of the expression the same?”
- “What is the resulting equivalent expression?”

**Synthesis**
- The goal of this discussion is for students to articulate how to rewrite very small numbers written using one power of 10 with a different power of 10.
- Invite students to share how they rewrote 29boldcdot 10^-7 using 10^-6. If not brought up in students’ explanations, consider asking the following series of questions:
- “What can 10^-7 be multiplied by to get 10^-6?” (10 because the exponent is increasing by 1)
- “If 10^-7 is multiplied by 10 to get 10^-6, the value of the expression will change. What can be done to keep the value of the expression the same?” (Divide the other factor by 10.)
- “What is the resulting equivalent expression?” (2.9boldcdot 10^-6)
- “How can 0.0123boldcdot10^-4 be written using 10^-6? (Since 10^-4 is being divided by 10^2, the factor 0.0123 needs to be multiplied by 10^2, resulting in 1.23boldcdot10^-6.)

## 15–35 min · Activity 11.3: Atomic Scale
*20 min · Partners*

**Purpose:** In this activity, students convert small decimals into a multiple of a power of 10 with a negative exponent for the first time.

**Launch**
- Ask students to think of different ways the decimal 0.03 can be written (3 hundredths, 3/100). If not brought up by students introduce the idea that 0.03 can also be written as 3boldcdot1/100 or 3boldcdot1/10boldcdot1/10. So 0.03=3boldcdot1/10boldcdot1/10=3boldcdot10^-2.
- Arrange students in groups of 2. Give students 10 minutes to work, followed by whole-class discussion.

**Students do**
- The radius of an electron is about 0.0000000000003 cm.
- Write this number as a multiple of a power of 10.
- Decide what power of 10 to put on the right side of this number line and label it.
- Label each tick mark as a multiple of a power of 10.
- [Image: A number line with eleven evenly spaced tick marks. The first tick is labeled 0, the last tick is labeled 10 to the blank power, and the remaining tick marks are blank.]
- Plot the radius of the electron in centimeters on the number line.
- The mass of a proton is about 0.0000000000000000000000017 grams.
- Write this number as a multiple of a power of 10.
- Decide what power of 10 to put on the right side of this number line and label it.
- Label each tick mark as a multiple of a power of 10.
- [Image: A number line with eleven evenly spaced tick marks. The first tick is labeled 0, the last tick is labeled 10 to the blank power, and the remaining tick marks are blank.]
- Plot the mass of the proton in grams on the number line.
- Point A on the zoomed-in number line describes the wavelength of a certain X-ray in meters.
- [Image: A number line.]
- A number line, 11 tick marks, 0, 1 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 2 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 3 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 4 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 5 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 6 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 7 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 8 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 9 times 10 to the negative 12 power, 10 to the negative 11 power. - Write the wavelength of the X-ray as a multiple of a power of 10.
- Write the wavelength of the X-ray as a decimal.

**If students struggle**
- If students write the mass of a proton as 17 boldcdot 10^-25 but find that there are not enough tick marks on the number line, consider asking:
- “Would 1.7 fit on the given number line?” (Yes.)
- “How could the expression be written with 1.7 as one of the factors while still keeping the value of the expression the same?” (Divide 17 by 10 and multiply 10^-25 by 10, resulting in (1.7) boldcdot 10^-24.

**Ask**
- “What is 0.00014 written as a multiple of 10^-5?”
- “What is 0.0000008 written as a multiple of 10^-7?”
- “What is 0.00256 written as a multiple of 10^-3?”

**Synthesis**
- The goal of this discussion is for students to share the strategies they used when writing small decimals as a multiple of a power of 10. Some students may describe a strategy where they “move” the decimal point or count decimal places. Encourage students to develop a more precise explanation by emphasizing that each time a number is multiplied by 1/10, its value decreases by one place value. So for example, the value 0.0000003 is equal to 3boldcdot10^-7 because 3 has been multiplied by 1/10 seven times.
- If time allows, have students practice writing decimals as a multiple of a given power of 10. Note that students are not expected to write these values using scientific notation at this time.
- “What is 0.00014 written as a multiple of 10^-5?” (14boldcdot10^-5)
- “What is 0.0000008 written as a multiple of 10^-7?” (8boldcdot10^-7)
- “What is 0.00256 written as a multiple of 10^-3?” ((2.56)boldcdot10^-3)

**Access supports**
- Action and Expression: Internalize Executive Functions. Chunk this task into more manageable parts to support use of structure. For example, check in with students within the first 2-3 minutes of work time. Ask students to share how they decide what power of 10 to put on the right side of this number line.

## 35–40 min · Lesson Synthesis
*5 min\* · Whole class*

**Purpose:** The purpose of this discussion is to compare representing very small numbers with representing very large numbers using multiples of powers of 10 or on a number line.

**Ask**
- “Given two points on the number line, how can we tell which point represents a larger value?”
- “Would 10^5 appear to the left or to the right of 10^4 on a number line? Why?”
- “Would 10^-5 appear to the left or to the right of 10^-4 on a number line? Why?”
- “How does zooming in on the number line help express numbers between the tick marks?”

**Synthesis**
- The purpose of this discussion is to compare representing very small numbers with representing very large numbers using multiples of powers of 10 or on a number line. One important concept to highlight is that it’s always possible to change an expression that is a multiple of a power of 10 so that the leading factor is between 1 and 10.
- Here are some questions for discussion:
- “Given two points on the number line, how can we tell which point represents a larger value?” (The number on the right will always be larger.)
- “Would 10^5 appear to the left or to the right of 10^4 on a number line? Why?” (10^5 is larger than 10^4, so it would be to the right.)
- “Would 10^-5 appear to the left or to the right of 10^-4 on a number line? Why?” (10^-5 is smaller than 10^-4, so it would be to the left.)
- “How does zooming in on the number line help express numbers between the tick marks?” (Zooming in allows us to subdivide the distance between two tick marks into 10 equal intervals, which allows us to describe a number to an additional level of precision.)
- “Describe how to convert a number such as 0.000278 into a multiple of a power of 10.” (The number is equivalent to 278 boldcdot (0.000001) or 278 boldcdot 1/(100,000). The fraction 1/(100,000) is 1/(10^6) or 10^-6, so 0.000278 can be written as 278 boldcdot 10^-6.)
- If time allows, give students other small numbers that are written as decimals and ask them to write them as multiples of powers of 10, and vice versa.
